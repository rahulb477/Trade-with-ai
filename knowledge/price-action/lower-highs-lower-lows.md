# Lower Highs and Lower Lows (Downtrend Structure)

## 1. Definition
A downtrend is a sequence of **lower highs (LH)** and **lower lows (LL)**. Both are required.

```
   LH ╲              LH
        ╲    ╱╲    ╱
         ╲  ╱  ╲  ╱
          ╲╱    ╲╱
           LL     LL
```

## 2. Identification
Same method as for uptrends: fix `k`, mark swings, check monotonicity. Three or more LH/LL pairs constitute a
structure; one or two is a lean.

## 3. What it tells you

- Supply is progressively more aggressive: sellers accept lower prices, and buyers cannot push price back to
  the previous high.
- The **most recent lower high is the invalidation reference**: a decisive close above it is the first
  structural evidence that the downtrend has failed.
- Rallies into a lower high are the classic short location — not breakdowns at the lows.

## 4. Where it is actionable

| Location | Quality |
|----------|---------|
| Rally into the last LH, with a rejection candle | Highest |
| Rally to a falling trendline through the LHs | High |
| Rally to a moving average coinciding with an LH | High |
| Breakdown below the last LL | Medium (chasing; and often a trap) |
| Mid-way between LH and LL | Low |

## 5. Invalidation

| Event | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Close above the most recent LH | A higher high has formed — the downtrend structure is broken |
| An HL forms after an LL | First warning of a change |
| Close above the LH and a successful retest as support | Reversal confirmed structurally |

## 6. Asymmetry you should know about

Downtrends behave differently from uptrends, for documented structural reasons:

1. **Declines are faster.** Panic and margin calls compress time. This means bear flags and bearish
   continuations can resolve quickly, and stops get run through.
2. **Bounces are sharp.** Short covering produces violent counter-trend rallies, which is why shorting a
   breakdown at the low is often a losing trade and shorting the *rally into resistance* is better.
3. **Volume patterns differ.** Bottoms often show capitulation spikes; tops often show quiet distribution.

Do not assume a downtrend is an uptrend mirrored. It is not.

## 7. Common errors

| Error | Guard |
|-------|-------|
| Shorting a breakdown at the low | Wait for the retest of the broken level from below |
| Ignoring that the higher timeframe may be up | Check one timeframe up |
| Counting a marginally lower high as an LH | Require a meaningful margin (≥0.2 ATR) |
| Treating a violent bounce as a reversal | Require a close above the LH |
| Assuming the trend must continue because it "looks weak" | Structure only describes the past |

## 8. Beginner summary (Hinglish)

**Downtrend:** har naya high pichhle se neeche (LH), aur har naya low pichhle se neeche (LL).

**Sabse important level:** **last lower high**. Agar price uske upar **close** kar de, to downtrend ka structure
toot gaya.

**Short kahan?** LL par nahi — **LH par rally** mein, rejection candle ke saath. Kyunki LL par short karna
aksar trap hota hai (short covering se tez bounce aata hai).

**Yaad rakho:** downtrend uptrend ka mirror nahi hai — girawat tez hoti hai, bounce violent hote hain.

## 9. Exercise

On a daily chart in a downtrend, mark the LH levels. For each of the last three, record the distance from the
LH in ATR and what happened when price reached it. Then check: how many times would shorting the *LL breakdown*
have worked versus shorting the *LH rejection*? Write the comparison down.
