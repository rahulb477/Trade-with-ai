# Trend Continuation

## 1. Definition
**Continuation** is the resumption of an established trend after a pause or pullback. The trend's structure
(HH/HL or LH/LL) remains intact throughout.

## 2. The continuation sequence

```
1. Impulse        — a strong directional leg (wide-range bars, volume expansion)
2. Pullback       — a counter-trend drift, usually on lower volume, retracing 23.6–61.8%
3. Hold           — price stops at a reference (prior swing, MA, Fib, breakout retest) without breaking structure
4. Trigger        — a close back in the trend direction, breaking the pullback's structure
5. Continuation   — a new HH (or LL) extends the trend
```

Every continuation setup is really a bet that step 3 holds and step 4 occurs. The invalidation is defined by
where step 3 would be proven wrong.

## 3. Quality checklist

| Check | Good |
|-------|------|
| Structure intact | No close beyond the last HL (uptrend) |
| Pullback depth | 23.6–50% of the impulse; >61.8% is a warning |
| Pullback volume | Lower than impulse volume |
| Hold location | At a confluence: prior swing, EMA, Fib zone, breakout retest |
| Trigger | Close beyond the pullback's high (uptrend) with volume |
| Higher timeframe | Aligned, or at worst neutral |
| Room | ≥2 ATR to the next resistance |

## 4. Continuation patterns

These are the pattern-level expressions of the above: bull flag, bear flag, pennant, ascending triangle,
descending triangle, rectangle (in trend), rising three methods, falling three methods.

## 5. Entry approaches

| Approach | Entry | Invalidation | Note |
|----------|-------|--------------|------|
| Pullback-to-reference | At the reference, on a rejection candle | Beyond the reference | Best R:R |
| Break of the pullback high | On the close above it | Below the pullback low | More confirmation, worse R:R |
| Inside the pullback | Early | Tight | Highest failure rate |
| Add-on after a new HH | On the breakout | Below the last HL | Late; poor R:R |

## 6. Why continuation is generally the higher-probability bet

Two documented reasons:

1. **Trends persist more often than they reverse** on any given bar, which is the momentum/autocorrelation effect
   behind trend-following.
2. **The pullback provides a low-risk entry**, because the invalidation is close and structurally defined.

Neither statement is a guarantee. Continuations fail, and when they do, the failure often becomes a reversal. The
reason to prefer continuation is *the location of the invalidation*, not a promise about direction.

## 7. Failure signals

| Signal | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Pullback exceeds 61.8% and closes there | The pullback is becoming a reversal |
| Close beyond the last HL | Structure broken |
| Pullback on *rising* volume | Distribution, not rest |
| Repeated failed breakouts of the pullback high | Demand exhausted |
| Lower high after the pullback | Structure turning |

## 8. Common errors

| Error | Guard |
|-------|-------|
| Buying the impulse's high instead of the pullback | Bad location, bad R:R |
| Entering before the pullback has finished | Wait for the hold |
| Treating every pullback as a continuation | Some are reversals; require the structure to hold |
| Ignoring the higher timeframe's own pullback | Nested pullbacks can compound |
| No defined trigger | A "feeling" is not a trigger |

## 9. Beginner summary (Hinglish)

**Continuation kya hai?** Trend chal raha hai, beech mein thoda pullback aaya, aur phir trend **resume** ho gaya.

**Sequence yaad rakho:** tez move (impulse) → pullback (kam volume) → reference par hold → trigger (close back in
trend direction) → naya high.

**Sabse achha entry:** pullback ke reference par (prior swing / EMA / Fib), rejection candle ke saath. Stop wahin
paas hota hai.

**Kab continuation fail hota hai?** Jab pullback 61.8% se zyada chala jaye, ya volume pullback mein **badh**
jaye, ya price last HL ke neeche close kar de.

**Yaad rakho:** continuation "higher probability" isliye hai kyunki stop paas hota hai — direction ki guarantee
isliye nahi.
