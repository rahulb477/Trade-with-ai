# Financial Health — The Red Flags Relevant to a Chart Analyst

You are not doing a credit analysis. You are looking for reasons a chart might behave abnormally — because a
company with balance-sheet stress can gap, be halted, or trend in ways its technicals do not explain.

## 1. The six checks

| Check | Where | Red flag |
|-------|-------|----------|
| **Leverage** | Net debt / EBITDA | > 3–4× in a cyclical; rising fast |
| **Interest coverage** | EBIT / interest | < 2× |
| **Cash conversion** | Operating cash flow / net profit | Persistently < 0.7 |
| **Receivables** | Debtor days trend | Rising sharply while revenue grows slowly |
| **Promoter/insider holding** | Shareholding pattern | Sustained pledging or falling stake |
| **Related-party transactions** | Annual report notes | Large or opaque |

These are **screening** checks, not a verdict. A high-debt company can be a fine trade; you just need to know that
its chart has different tail risk.

## 2. Why a chart analyst should care

| Fundamental condition | Chart consequence |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| High leverage + rising rates | Sharp, news-driven moves; trend fragility |
| Negative cash flow + growth capex | Dilution risk (QIP/preferential issue) → supply overhang |
| Promoter pledging | Forced-selling risk → sudden gaps down |
| Low free float | Erratic moves, wide spreads, poor fills |
| Circuit-prone stock (illiquid) | Stops do not fill; gap risk is extreme |
| Pending regulatory action | Binary event risk |
| Recent IPO with lock-in expiry | Supply event on a known date |

These belong in the report's context, with a source and date — never asserted from memory.

## 3. What to say and what not to say

> ✅ "Per the Q2 FY26 filing, net debt/EBITDA is 3.8× and interest coverage 1.9×. That is elevated for this
> sector. Practical implication for the chart: news about refinancing or rates can produce outsized moves, so
> gap risk is higher than the ATR suggests."
>
> ❌ "The company is in trouble."

The first is a cited fact plus an implication. The second is an unsupported judgement about solvency.

## 4. Dilution and supply overhang

```
new_shares / existing_shares = dilution %
adjusted EPS = old EPS × existing / (existing + new)
```

Practical chart consequence: a company that repeatedly raises equity has a persistent supply of new shares. When
lock-in periods expire, that supply can hit the market on a known date.

## 5. Data sources (India)

| Data | Source |
|------|--------|
| Quarterly and annual results | Company filings, exchange announcements |
| Shareholding pattern | Exchange filings (quarterly) |
| Corporate actions | Exchange notices |
| Credit ratings | Rating agency press releases |
| Related-party and pledge details | Annual report notes, shareholding pattern |

All of these must be **cited with a date**. None should be reconstructed from memory.

## 6. Beginner summary (Hinglish)

Aap fundamental analyst nahi ho — bas **red flags** dekh rahe ho jo chart ka behaviour badal dete hain.

**Chhe checks:**
1. **Debt** (net debt/EBITDA) zyada?
2. **Interest coverage** 2× se kam?
3. **Cash flow** profit se kaafi kam?
4. **Receivables** tezi se badh rahe?
5. **Promoter holding** gir rahi hai ya pledge badh raha hai?
6. **Related-party transactions** bade hain?

**Chart ke liye kyun matter karta hai:**
- Zyada debt + rate news = bade jumps.
- Cash flow negative + capex = dilution (naye shares) = supply.
- Pledging = forced selling = achanak gap down.
- Low float / illiquid = stop fill nahi hota.

**Kya na likho:** "company trouble mein hai". **Kya likho:** "filing ke hisaab se debt/EBITDA 3.8× hai — isliye
gap risk ATR se zyada ho sakta hai."

## 7. Exercise

Pick one stock and look up net debt/EBITDA, interest coverage, and operating cash flow/net profit for the last
three years. Then look at its daily chart. Do the largest single-day gaps line up with any fundamental stress
period? Write two sentences — one citing the fact, one describing the chart implication.
